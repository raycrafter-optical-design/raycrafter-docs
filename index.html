What is RayCrafter?

RayCrafter is simple software for tracing rays through optical systems built from off-the-shelf parts. You pick lenses from a catalogue, drop them on an optical axis, drag them around, and watch the result update as you move them.

Very early stages of development. RayCrafter is pre-alpha. It has bugs, features are missing, and anything may change, including file formats. Please check results against other tools before you rely on them. See known problems.

What it is

  • Built around real, off-the-shelf lenses. The full Thorlabs catalogue (spherical lenses, achromats, air-spaced doublets, triplets and matched pairs) is built in, plus ideal (paraxial) lenses with a focal length you choose.
  • Not surface based. You do not enter radii, thicknesses and glasses one surface at a time. You place whole lenses.
  • Not lens design. RayCrafter does not optimise or invent lenses. It helps you work out how a set of lenses you could buy will behave together: spacing, focus, aberrations, image quality.
  • Easy to use. Drag lenses, flip them, make Plossl pairs, add beams (fields) with their own width, angle, vergence, offset and wavelength. Plots update live.
  • Cross platform. Runs on Windows, macOS and Linux.
  • Exports. Save and load Zemax .zmx files and write an HTML report.

Built on Optiland

The ray tracing itself is done by Optiland, an open-source Python optical design library. RayCrafter is a simple front end for it, aimed at people who want to build and understand a lens system rather than design one from scratch.
